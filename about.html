About RPIND

Born in the creeks. Built for peace. Trusted by communities, government and the world.

Our Story

Riverine Peace Initiative in Niger Delta (RPIND) was born out of pain — years of militancy, oil spills, kidnappings, and neglect in the riverine communities.

While others chose violence, RPIND chose dialogue. We went into the creeks, sat with aggrieved youths, spoke with traditional rulers, oil companies and government officials, and said one thing: "There is a better way."

Today, RPIND is one of the most respected peace voices in the Niger Delta — credited with the Amnesty idea, the rescue of foreign nationals, and stopping many pipeline attacks before they happened.

Our Vision, Mission & Values

👁️ Vision

A peaceful, united and developed Niger Delta where riverine communities thrive without fear, violence or marginalization.

🎯 Mission

To sustain peace through dialogue, mediation, advocacy, rehabilitation and inclusive development for all stakeholders in the Niger Delta.

💎 Core Values

1. Dialogue over violence
2. Inclusion over exclusion
3. Community first
4. Courage & Accountability
5. Love for Niger Delta

Our Journey — Timeline

2009 - The Call For Amnesty: RPIND was among the first voices to champion an Amnesty Programme for Niger Delta militants — an idea later adopted by the Federal Government.
2016 - Pipeline Peace: RPIND mediated between government and aggrieved groups to prevent renewed pipeline bombings and protect national assets.
2019 - Turkish Sailors Rescued: 10 Turkish sailors kidnapped off Bayelsa were released without ransom after RPIND mediation. Featured on Channels TV.
2023 - Attack Suspended: After RPIND intervention, Isourhobo Bombers suspended planned attack on gas pipelines in Delta and chose negotiation.
2026 - Call For Inclusion: RPIND condemns keeping pipeline surveillance in hands of a few elites, demands inclusion of ex-militants and peace builders.
Today - Building The Future: RPIND continues to push for jobs, clean water, roads, education and real development for riverine communities.

Why Communities Trust RPIND

Because we don't just talk peace on TV. We go to the creeks. We sleep in the communities. We stand with the people.
